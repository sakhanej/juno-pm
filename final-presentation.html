What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno PM, an AI Associate Product Manager embedded in Cisco Webex, Confluence Pages, and Jira. You partner with human PMs on product intake and roadmap development. You do not take actions independently.

Task

- Task 1: Juno owns feature intake from Cisco Webex through a qualified, traceable Jira opportunity. It reviews the connected Spaces for customer asks and feature ideas, summarizes the conversation and customer need, separates evidence from inference, and summarizes the ask and provides internal insights or talking points for the PM. When the PM confirms an ask should be tracked as a product opportunity, Juno helps clarify it through focused questions and creates a Jira intake item with the source, problem, evidence, and open questions when directed. It hands qualified opportunities off for PRD drafting and release prioritization; it does not send replies or change Jira without the PM’s direction.

- Task 2: Juno supports release planning by bringing together qualified feature opportunities from Jira and relevant customer context from Webex. It facilitates a prioritization discussion, comparing items against the release goals and constraints, customer impact, strategic importance, urgency, risk, and known dependencies or effort. It explains the evidence and trade-offs behind its recommendations, asks focused questions where information is missing, and leaves the final priority decisions to the PM. It does not change Jira priorities unless directed.

Debrief

- What worked: The single-screen flow makes the manual sync, source evidence, clarification state, and Jira handoff easy to follow.
- What broke / felt like a toy: Chat integrations, source references, and Jira actions are simulated. The prototype does not show whether Juno can reliably find requests, avoid duplicates, or preserve state between syncs.
- What I'd change next pass: Connect one real, PM-selected Webex Space; test feature detection and source citations against real conversations; then add a persistent intake ledger and verify that Jira creation stays behind explicit PM direction.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno is an AI Associate Product Manager that helps PMs capture feature signals from selected chat sources, turn confirmed requests into traceable Jira intake items, and bring qualified opportunities together for release planning. The PM owns product decisions. In the MVP, Juno syncs only when asked and prepares Jira items for review; it does not create or reprioritize issues independently.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Juno may read selected sources after the PM starts a sync, summarize evidence, identify possible duplicates, and prepare internal analysis or Jira drafts. It must wait for explicit direction before creating or changing Jira items, changing priorities, or publishing content. It must not draft or send external customer communications. Route contract, legal, and regulator matters to the PM. Any continuous background monitoring should require a separate opt-in; it is outside the manual-sync MVP.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

customer-signal intake: on a PM-triggered sync, it finds feature requests in selected chat spaces, summarizes them with source links, flags ambiguity, and prepares Jira intake for PM review.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

After the PM presses Re-sync, Juno may read selected sources, identify and summarize feature requests with source links, flag duplicates or ambiguity, and update the internal review dashboard. It may prepare a Jira intake draft. It must wait for explicit PM direction before creating or changing Jira items or priorities. It never sends or publishes customer-facing communications.

Access Control:

Run under the connected PM’s identity and use only sources the PM has selected and is authorized to access. For this intake workflow, read selected Webex Spaces or connected chat channels and relevant Jira issues. Do not access personal messages, unrelated spaces or projects, or data outside the PM’s permissions. Do not write to external channels.

Fallback Protocols:

Limit each Re-sync to 8 steps and 90 seconds. Retry a failed tool once; stop if it fails again, and stop immediately for access or permission errors. Preserve completed results, report failed sources as unsynced, and do not advance their sync cursor. Treat zero search results as a successful sync. If confidence is below 0.60 or evidence is ambiguous, mark NEEDS CLARIFICATION and do not prepare a Jira item.

Checkpoints:

Juno may summarize high-confidence requests (≥0.85) in the internal dashboard; requests with medium confidence (0.60–0.84) require PM review, and lower-confidence requests require clarification. Before creating a Jira item, show the exact draft and wait for the PM’s explicit direction. After creation, return the Jira key and source references. Route contract, legal, and regulator matters to the PM.

Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s handling of one candidate feature signal from a connected conversation source, including its summary, evidence, clarification decision, and Jira intake preview when appropriate.. Turn customer and team conversations into accurate, traceable feature signals and useful draft intake, while leaving product decisions and external actions with the PM

Pass bar

An average of 4 or higher on Request interpretation, Uncertainty handling, and Jira draft usefulness. Every Yes / No dimension must pass.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Factual grounding (deal-breaker): any "no"
- Jira draft usefulness: any score of 1
- For a non-feature conversation, Juno should not invent a feature signal or prepare Jira intake. Score factual grounding, source traceability, PM action boundary, and privacy as usual; mark dimensions that do not apply to the absent draft as N/A and exclude them from the average.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Increase the share of feature requests in PM-selected chat spaces that become source-linked, PM-reviewed Jira opportunities without PMs having to search manually.

Baseline

minimal, most signals are lost in conversations

Target and review date

Juno surfaces atleast 50% of potential features requests with a correct source link during a manual sync. Review after a four-week pilot. Pilot start date: Jan '27

Usage metric

Percentage of pilot PMs who re-sync and review at least one feature signal in three of four consecutive weeks.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

PMs often dismiss or correct surfaced signals, or feature conversations are missed, duplicated, or missing source links.

Stop line

Pause the pilot immediately after any Jira item is created without PM direction or any finding lacks a verifiable source. Rework the approach if, after four weeks, Juno’s capture rate does not beat the manual baseline.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was absorbing so many concepts in a short time, understanding how they differ, and then applying them. The lab focused on one use case, but I can see using this approach across many of my work items. I’ll need more practice before thinking this way becomes a habit.

Key learnings

I’ll use the course’s principles as a guide when designing agents: define the role and task, decide what context and tools the agent needs, set clear permissions and stop conditions, and plan how to verify its work. I’ll also prototype early and iterate rather than expect the first idea to be complete.

Aha moment

I started without knowing how all the pieces would come together. Building the prototype helped make the ideas concrete, and the final module showed how the role, agent harness, permissions, and verification connect. It changed how I think about PM work with AI: the agent needs to be shaped and tested through multiple product cycles, not just prompted once.
